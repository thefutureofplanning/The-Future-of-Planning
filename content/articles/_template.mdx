Open with the observation, not the throat-clearing. One or two paragraphs that put the reader inside the problem before any structure appears.

## The first idea

Use h2 for the main moves in the argument; they become the table of contents automatically. Keep them short — three to six words reads best in the sidebar.

### A sub-point

h3 nests under the previous h2 in the contents list. Use it sparingly.

<PullQuote>
One line, once per piece. Save it for the sentence you would want quoted back to you.
</PullQuote>

## The second idea

Ordinary markdown works throughout: **bold**, _italic_, [links](/articles), lists, and tables.

<Note label="A caveat">
Use Note for the aside that would otherwise interrupt the argument — a qualification, a piece of context, a disclosure.
</Note>

## Where this leaves us

End on the implication rather than a summary. The reader already read it.
